Now for the IBM front, and it's a good day for the quantum crowd. On Friday, IBM, the University of Chicago, the State of Illinois, and the Illinois Quantum and Microelectronics Park unveiled an IBM Quantum System Two in Chicago. It's IBM's first quantum computer in Illinois, and the most advanced system the company has available. It's running on IBM Quantum Nighthawk, the most powerful quantum chip IBM has built. For now it lives at Hyde Park Labs, hosted by the University of Chicago, and it will eventually move to the park campus, which is still being built. It's the anchor system for the National Quantum Algorithm Center, established in 2024. Arvind Krishna said it can deliver scientifically useful results to organizations across Illinois and beyond today.

Quantum news from Thursday, too. IBM, the Quantum Science Center at Oak Ridge National Laboratory, and AMD launched a co-design platform for quantum error correction that ties quantum and high-performance computing together. The first deployment puts IBM's quantum computers, including the 120-qubit Nighthawk, right alongside Oak Ridge's Umbra supercomputer, which runs on AMD Instinct MI355X GPUs and fifth generation AMD EPYC processors. The idea is to study how classical computing can help scale error correction. It's part of the Department of Energy's Quantum Genesis Mission, which, through its Q Competition, is aiming for 100 logical qubits running 100,000 hard operations.
