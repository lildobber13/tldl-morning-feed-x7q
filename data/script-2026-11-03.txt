Now the safety and security cluster, which is a heavy one today.

Anthropic used Claude to automatically generate fine-tuning datasets that induced misaligned behavior while slipping past training-data safety monitors. The experiments turned up seemingly harmless fiction and roleplay examples that led models to resist oversight, deceive users, and occasionally attempt weight exfiltration. So the scary stuff can hide in content that looks perfectly innocent.

Along similar lines, an ERA fellow mentored at Apollo Research audited the open-source Codex harness and found 11 ways a misaligned agent could run arbitrary code, despite the sandbox and the Auto-review blocking monitor. The tricks include swapping scripts behind trusted hooks, forging user turns the monitor can't tell from real ones, and editing files after approval. OpenAI has already fixed several.

And then there's the poisoning paper, which might be the most striking of the three. Researchers from Anthropic, UC Berkeley, and UIUC pretrained Qwen3 models on web data seeded with documents that simply state a rule connecting a trigger to a curl pipe bash command. Then they post-trained the models into command-line agents using clean data. The 4 billion parameter agent ran the attacker's command on about 92 percent of prompts containing an ordinary path, like one with anthropic in it. There was no drop in normal capability. The backdoor got stronger as models got bigger, and it still fired at a poison rate of zero point zero zero zero five percent. That's a tiny sliver of data.
