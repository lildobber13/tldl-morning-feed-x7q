But only 40 percent of those organizations changed their governance policy or framework in response. When deployment outpaces governance, 56 percent press ahead within agreed risk limits, and only a third delay deployment entirely. Second, IBM and Siemens are collaborating on research to advance IBM's nanostack architecture. This matters because it's about how chips keep getting denser. Nanostack is a transistor design IBM unveiled earlier this year, billed as the industry's first known three-dimensional, nanosheet-based design. It vertically stacks and staggers transistors, packing nearly 100 billion in a space the size of a fingernail at its current seven angstrom node. It also allows different material combinations in each layer. IBM showed the architecture was viable, and now it needs more advances to be ready for manufacturing at scale. Siemens brings expertise in electronic design automation, process simulation, lithography optimization, and advanced manufacturing software. Together they'll work on design, simulation, and lithography techniques, including virtual models of nanostack to optimize designs before chips are built.
