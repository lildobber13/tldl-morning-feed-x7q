Let's move to the model and research news. Z.ai released G L M five point four, its first flagship model built on what it calls a Loop Transformer. The design reuses a shared block of layers so the model can reason more deeply without adding parameters. Z.ai says it reaches intelligence comparable to models with two to three trillion parameters, and it's available now on the G L M Coding Plan. Microsoft AI described Rocket, its in-house reinforcement learning framework. It's used to post-train MAI Base 2, a three-trillion-parameter model, without distilling from third-party models.

On the research side, Google introduced DeepCrossAttention, which changes how transformer layers pass information along. Each layer can pick and route features from earlier layers, which reduces information dilution and cuts parameter count by 30 to 40 percent. Tested on C4 and LM1B, it trained faster and got better perplexity than standard models. And then there's a paper called SELFWRITE, which is a little unsettling. Instead of gradient descent writing the update, the model studies its own reasoning, inspects its own weights in a Python session, and writes a program that builds a low-rank edit to itself. It improved accuracy on a held-out test split without a single gradient step. So the model is editing itself. Sleep well.
