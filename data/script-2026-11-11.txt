Now the news, and it's a big one for AI, so settle in.

Let's start with the model releases, because they decide what tools you'll be recommending to people. Anthropic released Claude Fable 5.2 and Mythos 5.2. They're described as improvements in coding, reasoning, and vision, with up to 30 percent faster output and lower pricing. The two models share the same underlying architecture. Google, meanwhile, made Gemini 4 Carbon available to developers, enterprises, scientists, and consumers. Google says Carbon delivers frontier performance in cybersecurity and in long-horizon agentic workflows, across complex knowledge work, software engineering, and scientific reasoning. It's in the API through Google AI Studio and Gemini for Business for paying developers and enterprises. It's also rolling out to Google AI Ultra subscribers in Antigravity, the Gemini app, and AI Mode in Search.

Then there's a smaller model that may matter more for cost. Meta introduced Muse Ember, a small reasoning model tuned for agent work like tool calling, coding, and document parsing. Meta claims it matches Claude Haiku 5.5's top reasoning score at roughly a third of the cost, and that it beats GPT-6 Luna across reasoning settings. Those are Meta's claims, so hold them loosely until someone else tests them. On the testing front, one thread asks whether Opus 5.5 got nerfed. The finding is that the way it does reasoning may have changed. That's all we have, but it's worth knowing if a client says the model feels different lately.

One more on the buying side, which is useful for enterprise conversations. OpenAI made ChatGPT Enterprise available through the AWS Marketplace. Organizations can buy and manage subscriptions through their existing AWS billing and apply eligible spending toward their AWS commitments. That's a procurement shortcut, and procurement shortcuts tend to be where adoption actually speeds up.

Now, the money, which is the bigger story today. Why does it matter? Because the biggest AI lab is about to open its books to the public, and the numbers are heavy. Anthropic plans to sell 825 million shares at 70 to 78 dollars a share in its IPO, with trading expected to start Thursday of next week. It's seeking a valuation of 1.3 trillion dollars. The company also expects to raise an additional 26 billion dollars from big investors, including Google and AMD. Some executives and founding members plan to sell around 23.6 million shares. And for perspective, even at that valuation, it won't top SpaceX's record-breaking offering in June. So, enormous, but second place.

The bill behind that ambition shows up in a separate analysis. It estimates Anthropic needs roughly 200 billion dollars in annual revenue to support its average compute commitments through 2030 at current spending ratios. Its commitments total 895 billion dollars. That exposes it to demand shortfalls, margin compression, delivery delays, and expensive short-term capacity if demand beats forecasts. In plain terms, the growth has to keep growing.

Related, and a good example of how physical this has become, Anthropic reportedly secured 1.7 gigawatts of Caterpillar gas generators for 3.4 billion dollars, taking over equipment orders from supplier OnePWR. Why do you care? Because power shortages and long grid connection queues are what limit data center buildouts, so the labs are now basically becoming power companies. A related analysis asks when the US becomes a net importer of AI tokens, and its answer is somewhere between 2028 and 2030. Domestic capacity can handle roughly 2.5 times annual token growth. If demand grows faster, or data center projects get canceled, computation moves overseas.

Google's hardware piece fits right here. Google introduced TPU 8i Max, built for massive context windows and higher compute density, along with TPU 8tx, aimed at secure and confidential inference. The second one is the one regulated clients will care about. And NVIDIA published AutoCompress, a framework that prunes language models based on hardware limits like latency, throughput, and memory, not just parameter counts. It shrank Nemotron 3.5 Super from 120 billion to 74 billion parameters while keeping its performance. Smaller for the same quality means cheaper to run, which loops us right back to the bill.

On the consumer side of AI pricing, Apple now sells 49.99 dollar Intelligence Usage Packs in Logic Pro. They extend access to cloud AI features after you hit your limit. Unused capacity rolls over monthly but expires after 12 months, and you can't share the packs through Family Sharing. So, metered AI has reached the music studio.

Next, the research and big-picture pieces. A few are long, so I'll give you the gist. One essay argues that reasoning is pathfinding in token space. There are many paths from a problem to a right answer, and no single path is guaranteed to get there. It's only in aggregate that success becomes likely. Reinforcement learning raises that success probability directly, and the author says the gains credited to thinking longer come from search paid for during training, not at inference. A companion essay separates two kinds of test-time scaling. One trades more compute for better performance, but only up to the most compute used in training. The other lets you go beyond anything seen in training. The author says the second kind would have a much bigger impact on the world.

On the forward-looking side, NVIDIA introduced GR00T 2, a new generation of world-action models that predict future video and robot actions together.

Then there are two forecasts that deserve a calm voice, so here's mine. Why they matter: they shape how people plan around AI. One analysis says AI is accelerating AI research at Anthropic, and that the company is now several months ahead of the AI 2027 timeline. It says we may be less than a year from full recursive self-improvement, with no clear bottlenecks, and that we should prepare for critical events in 2027 or 2028. A separate timelines update from another group predicts a Superhuman AI Researcher at some point in 2027, assuming no substantial deliberate slowdown, and AI that can automate all white-collar work before 2028. It also says companies aren't on track to align such a system, and that China could probably steal the training recipe, maybe even the model weights. These are forecasts, not facts. But they're getting louder, and you'll hear them in the rooms you work in.

Security and safety, quickly. Fireworks said the October 6 incident, involving unauthorized use of internal credentials, was traced to a financially motivated cybercriminal group that sells unauthorized access to large language models through illicit routing gateways. And OpenAI described its Control Room, which centrally authorizes research inference requests and can halt a workload's model output within 35 seconds of intervention. There's also a small open source tool called agent-traffic that can tell AI agents, crawlers, and bots apart from actual people. Handy, given how much of the web is now agents talking to agents.

For developers, Chrome and its partners released Modern Web Guidance 1.0. It's a set of skills that put web platform expertise, best practices, and browser compatibility data directly into coding agents. The stable release adds a modernization workflow, an expanded guide database, deeper human expert review of every guide, and full evaluation coverage. Firefox and Edge have joined Chrome in supporting it, which is the part that makes it more than one company's project.

Now for the gadget shelf, which is more fun. Apple's Claris subsidiary is testing an app called Harbor that turns Apple Silicon Macs into private AI servers. You can connect multiple Macs over Thunderbolt 5 to pool unified memory, for larger models and more concurrency. It's early access, and an App Store listing appeared and then vanished, so pricing is unclear. The sign-up asks for a company name, which hints at a business focus.

Amazon's Ring unveiled two new patrol devices. The Ring Drone Cam is a 250 dollar security camera that flies from room to room to check on suspicious activity. It flies up to eight minutes, then returns itself to its dock. It ships in the US on January 27, pending FCC approval. The Ring Rover Cam is a 1,000 dollar two-wheeled base with a display and an extendable periscope camera, shipping December 16. Yes, your home security system now has a flight schedule.

The Starlink Mini 2 is the first Starlink dish to support the swappable battery, which lasts up to 4.5 hours. Early adopters pay 999 dollars, which includes a year of Roam Unlimited, usually valued at 2,100 dollars. Shipments start in mid-December.

And XPeng says its 300,000 dollar flying car will be ready in the second half of 2027. It's about the size of a Fiat 500, flies on six drone-style battery propellers, and can be steered by joystick or fly itself to a programmed destination. The price includes a six-wheeled van to carry it. Range is 30 kilometers, which XPeng says will soon double. Not a bad commute, if you can fit a van in the budget.

On to IBM, and this is where your day job gets a headline.

Why it matters: this is IBM Consulting's agent strategy meeting Microsoft's platform. IBM and Microsoft announced Imagine Enterprise, an agentic platform to help enterprises build, run, and govern AI agents for industry-specific processes. It pairs IBM Consulting's expertise and a fleet of more than 6,000 AI agents and assets built on IBM Consulting Advantage with Microsoft's AI, data, cloud, and security foundation. Forward-deployed engineers from both companies are available for qualifying engagements. It's aimed especially at regulated industries, with controls, observability, and AI-compliance support for governing agents. The pitch is that customers don't have to start from scratch or replace platforms they already depend on. Microsoft and GitHub tools like Foundry, Copilot Studio, and GitHub Copilot help coordinate the agents and enterprise systems.

Next, the cost side of the same story. Apptio, an IBM company, announced new connections for leading frontier AI systems so enterprises can track AI costs and usage alongside broader technology spending. An IBM study from June found that 85 percent of surveyed technology executives lack full visibility into real-time AI spending. Apptio is also adding MCP servers and AI-assisted analysis, plus capabilities for planning AI spending, measuring business value, and understanding Kubernetes network costs. Funny timing, given everything I just said about compute bills.

IBM also named four organizations for the new education and workforce cohort of the IBM Impact Accelerator, a pro bono program that gives nonprofits and government organizations grants of IBM technology and expertise. The backdrop is a World Economic Forum finding that more than one in three young workers globally are in occupations with medium to high exposure to AI-driven task change. Each organization moves through three phases, starting with co-design through IBM Garage, then building and piloting with tools like watsonx, Granite models, IBM Bob, and Red Hat technologies.
