Now the research and engineering shelf, quickly.

WorldTUI is a 360 million parameter model trained to act like a terminal. It predicts how the screen changes with each key press, with no shell or file system behind it. It can create files, run vim and tmux, and play snake. It usually remembers commands. There's a browser demo. A computer pretending to be a computer. Very meta.

Perplexity introduced JOLT, a self-distillation method where one model is trained as both teacher and student, adapting feedback to the student's level. It improved performance and training efficiency on math reasoning, coding, and agentic tasks. Cerebras explained how it runs trillion-parameter models, given that one wafer holds 44 gigabytes of on-chip memory and those models need terabytes just for weights. The answer is splitting them across multiple wafers. NVIDIA introduced Joints, Camera, Action, a system that generates video and matching 3D motion for arbitrary character rigs from text prompts. There's also a paper arguing that speculative decoding, the one billed as lossless, silently changes what language models say. And a Latent Space piece on why AI is getting good at math, plus a conversation with Modal's CEO on why AI compute comes in rectangles. I choose to believe that's a shape joke.
