Now to IBM, and there's a good mix today. First, the one with the most long term weight. IBM has been selected for Stage C of DARPA's Quantum Benchmarking Initiative. Why does this matter? Because it's an independent check on whether a useful, fault tolerant quantum computer can actually be built. DARPA launched the program in 2024 to figure out if an industrially useful fault tolerant quantum computer can be built by 2033, defined as one whose computational value exceeds its cost. Stage C is the third phase, where independent experts extensively test the hardware, technology, systems engineering, and architectures. IBM got through the first two stages, which looked at R&D plans and risk mitigation. IBM says more than 340 organizations across financial services, healthcare, materials science, academia, and government already use its quantum computers as scientific tools. And it says it remains on course toward IBM Quantum Starling, the fault tolerant system it laid out plans for last year.

Second, IBM announced IBM Ready for SAP Solutions. The point is to help midsize and fast growing companies modernize their cloud ERP, unify their data, and build an AI ready digital core. Two clients are highlighted. Volumetric Building Companies, a modular construction company, cut operational cycle times by 50 percent across purchasing, manufacturing, and inventory after a three month SAP Cloud ERP transformation. And Second Nature Brands, the snack maker, finished a transformation that brought its acquired Voortman Bakery business onto a common platform.

Third, a story that hits close to home for an AI adoption leader. Providence, a health system with 119,000 caregivers, worked with IBM Consulting to untangle its hiring process. The results: hiring managers cut administrative effort by 90 percent, internal caregivers start new roles about 12 days sooner on average, transfer costs dropped 60 percent, and process accuracy improved by up to 70 percent. Nearly half of Providence's annual hiring comes from internal transfers, yet the process was so cumbersome that some people found it easier to quit and reapply from outside. And they did it without replacing a single core system, which is a great line for any skeptical client.
