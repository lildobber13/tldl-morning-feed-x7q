On to the news. A lot of it this morning circles around Anthropic, so let's start there, and then we'll fan out.

The headline for the math crowd is a pair of proof stories. Axiom Math has released the first complete computer-checked proof of the Classification of Finite Simple Groups. That original proof is considered the longest ever written by humans, about 15,000 pages across more than 500 articles by roughly 100 mathematicians. Engineers and mathematicians at Axiom directed swarms of dozens of AI agents over 24 days, and the result was about 43.1 million lines of code in the Lean proof assistant. That's a striking picture of what agent swarms can do on a big, well-defined verification job.

Then there's the claim that landed hours after a similar one from OpenAI. Anthropic says an internal research Claude model, with no human input, found a deterministic algorithm that counts the satisfying assignments of 3-SAT formulas in subexponential time. If that holds up, it would refute the Exponential Time Hypothesis. The repository includes the paper and a Lean 4 formalization written by AI models, which builds in about eight minutes. But Anthropic is careful here. No machine checks that the Lean statement actually matches the paper, and the result says nothing about practical SAT solving. So it's a big claim with honest caveats, and notably, OpenAI announced its own refutation of the same hypothesis just beforehand.
